New Tax Regime vs Old: Which One Saves You More?

The new regime's lower slab rates look attractive on paper — but for many taxpayers with deductions, the old regime still wins. Here's how to actually work out which one is cheaper for you.

05 Jul 2026 4 min read J. Karthikeyan & Co. Income Tax

Every filing season, the same question lands on our desk: "Should I move to the new tax regime?" There's no single answer — it depends entirely on how many deductions you actually claim. This article walks through the decision framework we use with clients, so you can work out your own answer instead of guessing.

1. The Core Difference Between the Two Regimes

The new tax regime (default under Section 115BAC) offers lower slab rates but strips out most exemptions and deductions — no Section 80C, no HRA exemption, no home loan interest deduction on a self-occupied property, among others. The old regime keeps higher slab rates but lets you claim the full range of deductions you've likely been using for years.

In short: the new regime is a bet on simplicity; the old regime is a bet on your deductions being large enough to beat the rate difference.

2. Slab Rate Comparison at a Glance

Income SlabOld Regime RateNew Regime Rate
Up to ₹2.5L / ₹3LNilNil
Next slab5%5%
Middle slabs20%10% / 15%
Top slab30%30% (at a higher threshold)

The new regime's rates look lower slab-for-slab, but that comparison is misleading in isolation — it ignores what you lose in deductions to get there. The only way to know which is cheaper is to compute both for your actual numbers.

Don't compare tax rates. Compare your final tax payable under both regimes, computed on your actual income and actual deductions. That's the only comparison that matters.

3. Who Typically Benefits From Each Regime

The new regime usually wins for:

  • Salaried individuals with minimal 80C investments and no home loan.
  • Younger professionals who haven't yet built up insurance, PPF or ELSS commitments.
  • Taxpayers who prefer simplicity over annual proof-of-investment paperwork.

The old regime usually wins for:

  • Individuals claiming HRA exemption in high-rent cities.
  • Those with an active home loan claiming interest deduction under Section 24(b).
  • Taxpayers maximising 80C (PPF, ELSS, life insurance), 80D (health insurance) and NPS contributions together.

4. Key Points to Remember

  • Salaried individuals can switch between regimes every year when filing their return.
  • Those with business or professional income face restrictions on switching back to the old regime once they've opted out.
  • The new regime is now the default — you must actively elect the old regime if you want it.
  • The standard deduction applies under both regimes for salaried taxpayers, so it doesn't factor into the decision.
  • Employer TDS defaults to the new regime unless you inform your employer of your choice at the start of the year.

5. A Worked Example

Consider a salaried employee earning ₹12 lakh annually, claiming ₹1.5 lakh under 80C, ₹25,000 under 80D, and ₹1.8 lakh in home loan interest.

ItemOld RegimeNew Regime
Gross Income₹12,00,000₹12,00,000
Deductions Claimed₹3,55,000Not available
Taxable Income₹8,45,000₹12,00,000 (less standard deduction)
Approx. Tax PayableLower, due to deductionsHigher, despite lower rates

In this scenario, the deductions are large enough that the old regime results in meaningfully lower tax — even with its higher slab rates. Change the numbers (say, no home loan and minimal 80C), and the outcome flips in favour of the new regime.

Compliance Tip

If you're salaried, notify your employer of your regime choice at the start of the financial year — this determines your monthly TDS. You can still choose differently at the time of filing, but a mismatch can mean a large refund or a large final payment either way.

7. Common Mistakes We See

  • Choosing a regime based on last year's decision without recomputing for changed income or investments.
  • Forgetting that once business/professional income taxpayers opt out of the old regime, switching back has restrictions.
  • Comparing headline slab rates instead of computing actual tax payable on real numbers.
  • Not informing the employer of the regime choice, leading to TDS mismatches and cash-flow surprises.

Conclusion

The safest approach is to recompute the comparison every filing season rather than defaulting to whatever you chose last year.
