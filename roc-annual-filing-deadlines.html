ROC Annual Filing Deadlines You Cannot Afford To Miss

Missed ROC deadlines don't just attract a fine — they attract a fine that grows every single day. Here's the complete annual filing calendar and how to never miss it again.

02 Jun 2026 4 min read J. Karthikeyan & Co. Compliance

Every registered company in India has a fixed set of annual filings due with the Registrar of Companies (ROC), regardless of whether the company transacted any business during the year. The penalty structure is what catches most directors off guard — it isn't a flat fine, it's an additional fee that accrues for every day the filing stays pending. Here's the calendar, the consequences, and how to build a system that never lets a deadline slip.

1. The Annual ROC Filing Calendar

FormPurposeTypical Due Date
AOC-4Filing of financial statementsWithin 30 days of the AGM
MGT-7 / MGT-7AAnnual return (7A for small companies/OPCs)Within 60 days of the AGM
DIR-3 KYCAnnual KYC for every director with a DIN30th September each year
ADT-1Auditor appointment/re-appointment intimationWithin 15 days of the AGM
DPT-3Return of deposits and exempted transactions30th June each year

The AGM itself must generally be held within six months of the financial year-end, which is why most of these downstream deadlines cascade from that single date — miss the AGM timeline, and every subsequent filing date shifts with it.

A "NIL" year — no transactions, no activity — does not mean no filing obligation. Dormant companies are exactly the ones we see penalised most often, because no one is watching the calendar.

2. Why Missing These Deadlines Is Expensive

Unlike a flat late fee, ROC additional fees are structured to grow the longer a filing stays pending — which means a form that's 90 days late can cost dramatically more than one that's 10 days late. Beyond the direct cost, persistently non-compliant companies risk being flagged for "Active Non-Compliant" status, and directors can face disqualification from holding directorships in other companies if defaults continue across multiple companies.

3. Key Points to Remember

  • DIR-3 KYC applies to every director with a DIN, every year — including directors of dormant companies.
  • AOC-4 and MGT-7/7A deadlines are anchored to your AGM date, not a fixed calendar date — track your AGM date carefully.
  • Additional fees accrue daily — the cost of delay compounds, it doesn't stay flat.
  • Repeated defaults across filings can lead to director disqualification, not just a monetary penalty.
  • Dormant or NIL-activity companies still carry the same filing obligations as active ones.

4. A Practical Checklist to Never Miss a Deadline

  1. Fix your AGM date early in the financial year and work every downstream deadline backward from it.
  2. Maintain a compliance calendar with reminders set at least 30 days before each due date, not on the day itself.
  3. Assign clear ownership — one person (internal or your CA firm) responsible for tracking and filing, not a shared assumption.
  4. File DIR-3 KYC for every director by September each year, regardless of company activity level.
  5. Reconcile board minutes and financials well before the AGM so AOC-4 and MGT-7 aren't rushed at the last moment.
Compliance Tip

If your company has had no transactions this year, don't assume "NIL filing" means no filing at all — AOC-4, MGT-7/7A and DIR-3 KYC still need to be filed on time, even if every field shows zero.

5. Where We See Filings Slip Most Often

Company TypeCommon gapFix
Dormant / holding companiesAssuming no activity means no filing neededSame compliance calendar as active companies
Startups post-incorporationFirst AGM/filing cycle missed due to inexperienceOnboard a compliance calendar from day one
Family-run private companiesNo dedicated compliance ownerAssign filings to your CA firm as a standing engagement
Companies with recent director changesDIR-3 KYC missed for a newly appointed directorTrigger a KYC reminder on every DIN allotment

6. Common Mistakes We See

  • Assuming a dormant or NIL-activity company has no filing obligation.
  • Delaying the AGM, which cascades every downstream ROC deadline later than planned.
  • Missing DIR-3 KYC for a director who joined mid-year and wasn't added to the compliance tracker.
  • Treating ROC compliance as a once-a-year fire drill instead of an ongoing calendar item.

Conclusion

ROC deadlines aren't complicated — they're just numerous, cascading, and unforgiving of delay. A company that fixes its AGM date early and tracks every downstream filing from there rarely runs into trouble. A company that treats compliance as an afterthought usually discovers the cost of that decision one daily-accruing fee at a time.
